(a)¶

Let $X = G/\sqrt{n}$, where $G \in \mathbb{R}^{n\times n}$ has i.i.d. $\mathcal{N}(0,1)$ entries. Let the spin vector be $s \in \{-1,1\}^n$ and define the variance parameter as: $$ F(G)=\frac{1}{n}\log\sum_{s\in\{-1,1\}^n} \exp\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt n}s^\top Gs+h(s)\right). $$ Then $F(G)=Z$. For every $i,j$, $$ \begin{align*} \frac{\partial F}{\partial G_{ij}} &= \frac{1}{n\sqrt n} \frac{\sum_s s_i s_j\exp\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt n}s^\top Gs+h(s)\right)} {\sum_s\exp\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt n}s^\top Gs+h(s)\right)}. \end{align*} $$ The fraction is a weighted average of $s_is_j \in \{-1,1\}$, so $$ \left|\frac{\partial F}{\partial G_{ij}}\right| \leq \frac{1}{n\sqrt n}. $$ Therefore, $$ \begin{align*} \|\nabla F(G)\|_F^2 &= \sum_{i,j=1}^n\left(\frac{\partial F}{\partial G_{ij}}\right)^2 \\ &\leq n^2\left(\frac{1}{n\sqrt n}\right)^2 \\ &= \frac{1}{n}. \end{align*} $$ Thus, after identifying $G$ with $\operatorname{vec}(G) \in \mathbb{R}^{n^2}$, $F$ is $1/\sqrt n$-Lipschitz. By the TIS inequality, $$ P[F(G)-EF(G)\geq t] \leq \exp\left(-\frac{t^2}{2(1/n)}\right) = e^{-nt^2/2}. $$ Applying the same bound to $-F$ and taking a union bound gives $$ P[|Z-EZ|\geq t] \leq 2e^{-nt^2/2}, \qquad t\geq0. $$

def run_trial(seed):
    trial_rng = np.random.default_rng(seed)
    epsilon = sigma * trial_rng.standard_normal(n)
    y = mu + epsilon

    alpha_desc = alphas[::-1]
    with threadpool_limits(limits=1):
        _, coefficients, _ = lasso_path(
            X,
            y,
            alphas=alpha_desc,
            max_iter=10000,
            tol=1e-8
        )

    coefficients = coefficients[:, ::-1]
    prediction_errors = np.linalg.norm(
        X @ (coefficients - beta_star[:, None]),
        axis=0
    )
    return prediction_errors

errors = np.vstack(
    Parallel(n_jobs=8, backend="loky", verbose=5)(
        delayed(run_trial)(seed) for seed in noise_seeds
    )
)

i. Let $f(\epsilon)=a+\langle u,\epsilon\rangle$ with $\|u\|_2=1$ and $\epsilon\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^2I_n)$. Then $$ \langle u,\epsilon\rangle \sim \mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^2\|u\|_2^2) =\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^2), $$ so $$ \operatorname{sd}(f(\epsilon))=\sigma. $$ Now suppose $P$ is an orthogonal projection of rank $r$. By rotational invariance, $$ \|P\epsilon\|_2 \overset{d}{=} \sigma\chi_r. $$ Since $E[\chi_r^2]=r$ and $$ E[\chi_r]=\sqrt{2}\frac{\Gamma((r+1)/2)}{\Gamma(r/2)}, $$ we have $$ \begin{align*} \operatorname{Var}(\|P\epsilon\|_2) &=\sigma^2\left[r-2\left(\frac{\Gamma((r+1)/2)}{\Gamma(r/2)}\right)^2\right] \\ &\longrightarrow \frac{\sigma^2}{2} \end{align*} $$ as $r\to\infty$. Hence, for large rank, $$ \operatorname{sd}(\|P\epsilon\|_2)\approx\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt2}. $$

At $\lambda = \lambda_0$, the dominant shrinkage bias is approximately a fixed vector, so changes in the norm are mainly caused by the component of the noise in the direction of that fixed vector. Thus, $E_\lambda$ resembles the affine model $a+\langle u,\epsilon\rangle$. This matches the observed standard deviation $1.001842\approx\sigma = 1$.

At $\lambda = 0.1\lambda_0$, the LASSO selects many variables and fits a larger noise subspace. Then $E_\lambda$ more closely resembles $\|P\epsilon\|_2$, whose standard deviation is approximately $1/\sqrt2\approx0.7071$. The observed value $0.705715$ agrees closely with this prediction.

ii. At $\lambda=\lambda_0$ and $\sigma=1$, the Chebyshev bound is $$ \frac{1}{t^2}. $$ Setting this equal to $0.01$ gives $$ t_{\mathrm{Cheb}}=10. $$ The TIS bound is $$ 2e^{-t^2/2}. $$ Setting this equal to $0.01$ gives $$ \begin{align*} 2e^{-t^2/2}&=0.01,\\ -\frac{t^2}{2}&=\log(0.005),\\ t_{\mathrm{TIS}}&=\sqrt{2\log 200}\approx3.255. \end{align*} $$ For the empirical tail, the smallest observed threshold having tail probability 0.01 is the empirical 99th percentile of $|E_{\lambda_0}-EE_{\lambda_0}|$, which is approximately $$ t_{\mathrm{emp}}\approx2.626. $$ The empirical tail has the Gaussian-type shape predicted by TIS, while the Chebyshev bound has only polynomial decay and is much too loose. The Poincare inequality gives only the variance statement $\operatorname{sd}(E_\lambda)\leq\sigma$. TIS adds a dimension-free Gaussian tail bound, controlling rare deviations on the exponential-in-$t^2$ scale rather than only through the second moment.
